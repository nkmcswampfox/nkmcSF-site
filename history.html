The History of the Nam Knights of America MC

The Nam Knights of America Motorcycle Club (MC) was founded in July 1989 in Carlstadt, New Jersey, by a small group of Harley-Davidson riding combat veterans. Led by Jack Quigley, a Vietnam War platoon sergeant and retired Undersheriff, they established the club to recapture wartime brotherhood and support veterans in need.

The Origin

A Unique Brotherhood. The founding members shared a distinct dual identity as Vietnam War combat veterans and law enforcement officers. When seeking a motorcycle club that honored their military service, traditional veteran clubs rejected them due to their law enforcement backgrounds. Guided by the suggestion of an established MC, they created their own club.

“Nam” honors their service in the Vietnam War.

“Knights” symbolizes their dedication to law enforcement and a code of protection.

Evolution and Membership Expansion

Today, the organization welcomes:

  1. Veterans from all military eras.
  2. Active-duty military personnel.
  3. Law enforcement officers, active and retired.
  4. Patriots, who are dedicated community members sharing the club's values.

Growth into an International Organization

What began as a single New Jersey parent chapter has grown into an international movement. The club now spans over 80 chapters across multiple U.S. states, and Canada.

The Legacy of the Patch

The club's colors and patch directly reflect its historical roots. The original founding patch features a large, fierce dragon holding the Vietnam Service Ribbon. As membership grew, the club introduced a new version featuring the dragon holding the American flag. A manufacturing error caused the first run to be smaller, earning the nickname “Gecko.”

Local Spotlight

The Swamp Fox Chapter was officially founded on February 20th, 2015. Headquartered in the Greater Charleston area, the chapter supports military personnel, veterans, and law enforcement officers.

The Namesake

The chapter draws its name from Revolutionary War Hero General Francis Marion, nicknamed the “Swamp Fox” by the British.

A Decade of Impact

Celebrating its 10-year anniversary in February 2025, the chapter has become a staple of the Charleston community.
